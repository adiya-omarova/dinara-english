🧩 Grammar

Short, simple rules. Every example comes from your own lesson answers.

1Tenses

Past Simple: things that finished

Use it for finished actions in the past. You know when it happened (yesterday, last Monday, on Sunday).

Positive: verb + -ed (regular) or a special form (irregular)

I worked / I went / I woke up
Regular (+ed)Irregular (learn them!)
watch → watched
cook → cooked
stop → stopped		go → went
have → had
see → saw
choose → chose
make → made
feel → felt

Negative: didn’t + base verb   Question: Did + subject + base verb?

Last Monday I woke up early and went to work.I woke up early and went to work.
I didn’t want to do anything else.
Did you go out? — Who did you go with?
✔ I didn’t want to go.
✘ I didn’t wanted to go.
Remember: after did / didn’t the verb goes back to the base form. Only ONE verb shows the past.

В вопросе и отрицании past уже «живёт» в did — глагол остаётся простым: Did you go?

2Imaginary situations

Second Conditional: “If I had…, I would…”

Use it to talk about an imaginary or unlikely situation. You are dreaming or imagining, not stating a fact.
If + Past Simple, … would + base verb
If I had a whole evening free, I would meet a friend.If I had… (but I don’t have time now), I would meet…
I would go for a walk if the weather was good.

You can change the order. When if is in the middle, no comma:

I would feel calmer if I put my phone away.
✔ If I had time, I would go.
✘ If I would have time, I would go.
Remember: never use would right after if. Put would in the other part of the sentence.

Это «если бы…, то…». Русское «бы» = английское would, но после if ставим Past Simple, а не would.

3Polite & advice

Would: recommend, prefer, would like

Use would to sound polite, give advice or say what you want. It is softer than “I want / I recommend”.
PatternExample
I would recommend + -ingI would recommend going for a short walk.
I would like + to + verbI would like to go for a walk.
I would prefer + nounI would prefer a quiet place.
I’m up for + noun / -ingI’m up for that! Let’s go together.

Short form: I would = I’d, they would = they’d, she would = she’d

I think they’d enjoy having a break from their usual routine.
Useful: “That’s not really my cup of tea” = I don’t really like it.
4Connecting ideas

Linking words: make your speech flow

Linking words join ideas so you sound smooth. Learn these 8 and use them every time you speak.
WordMeaningExample
becausereason (почему)I chose a silly programme because I was tired.
soresult (поэтому)I saved the video so I wouldn’t forget it.
butcontrast (но)The coffee was great, but the service was slow.
howevercontrast, new sentenceHowever, I checked other reviews.
even thoughdespite this (хотя)I didn’t know it, even though it was close to home.
as opposed toone thing, not the otherI prefer quiet places as opposed to noisy places.
compared tocomparingCompared to a phone, a walk helps me relax more.
for examplegive an exampleFor example, I plan ten minutes but stay an hour.
Tip: but is in the middle of a sentence. However usually starts a new sentence with a comma after it: However, …
5Verb patterns

When two verbs are together, the second verb changes. Learn the groups, don’t guess.
Verb + -ingVerb + to + base

After a preposition (about, for, of, without) always use -ing: a break from working, a way of relaxing.
6Comparing

Comparatives: more, calmer, better

Use a comparative to say one thing has more of a quality than another. Add -er to short words, use more before long words.
Word typeRuleExample
Short (1 syllable)+ ercalm → calmer
Long (2+ syllables)more + adjectiverelaxed → more relaxed
Ends in -yy → iereasy → easier
Irregularlearn by heartgood → better, bad → worse
After the programme I felt calmer.
A walk helps me feel more relaxed than a phone.

Use than to compare two things: quieter than a party.

✔ more relaxed
✘ relaxeder / more calmer
7News & information

Passive: when the action matters more than who does it

Passive = be + past participle (3rd form). Use it when you don’t know who does the action, or it is not important.
be (is / are / was) + V3
We are bombarded by information every day.Who bombards us? Not important. Information does.
Fake news is designed to upset people.
Some people get fooled by fake photos.get + V3 is a common, informal passive.

Active → Passive

Active: Someone published the story.
Passive: The story was published.
Tip: use by only if the person or thing doing the action is important.
8Tenses

Present Perfect: past + connection to now

Use it when the time is not important or the past action still matters now. Past Simple = finished time. Present Perfect = connected to today.
have / has + V3 (past participle)
UseExample
Life experience (ever, never)I’ve never been to that café.
Result nowI’ve saved the video. (So I can find it now.)
Still true (for / since)I’ve lived here for five years / since 2020.
Not yet / already / justI haven’t tried it yet. I’ve just arrived.
Past Simple (finished time)
I went there last week.
Present Perfect (no time)
I’ve been there. It was nice.
Remember: never use yesterday, last week, in 2019 with Present Perfect.

Present Perfect — это «я уже/ещё не делал; у меня есть опыт; это продолжается до сих пор». Время действия не называем.

9Reporting

Reported speech: say what someone said

When you tell what someone said, move the tense one step back in time.
Direct (what she said)Reported
“The coffee is great.”She said the coffee was great.
“I like the café.”She said she liked the café.
“I went there.”She said she had gone there.
“I will come.”She said she would come.
“I can help.”She said she could help.
She said the service was a little slow.from her answer about the café video

Also change: I → she/he, today → that day, here → there, now → then.

Questions: no question order. “Where do you live?” → He asked where I lived. (not “where did I live”)
10Modals

Must, have to, should: obligation and advice

Modal verbs are followed by the base verb. No -s, no to (except have to).
MeaningWordExample
Strong needhave to / mustI had to pay close attention in the meeting.
Not necessarydon’t have toThey don’t need to make special plans.
Forbiddenmustn’tYou mustn’t trust every ad.
AdviceshouldYou should check the source.
Possibilitycould / mightWe could talk about our week.
Careful: mustn’t = forbidden. don’t have to = not necessary. They are different!

Past of must/have to: use had to. I had to work late.

«Не обязан» = don’t have to. «Нельзя» = mustn’t.

11Conditionals

Zero & First Conditional: real situations

Use first conditional for real, possible future situations. Use zero conditional for facts and general truths.
TypeFormulaExample
ZeroIf + present, presentIf I spend too much time online, I feel tired.
FirstIf + present, will + baseIf I check the source, I won’t get fooled.
Compare:
If I have time, I will go. (real possibility)
If I had time, I would go. (imaginary, see Second Conditional)

Useful words instead of if: unless (= if not), as long as, when.

I won’t believe it unless I see the source.
✔ If it rains, I’ll stay home.
✘ If it will rain, I stay home.
12Vocabulary grammar

Phrasal verbMeaningПо-русскиExample

14Sentence building

Relative clauses: who, which, that, where

A relative clause gives extra information about a noun. Use it to join two short sentences into one longer, smoother one.
WordUsed forExample
who / thatpeoplea friend who wants to relax
which / thatthingsunusual desserts that I wanted to try
whereplacesa place where you can read
The first thing that caught my attention was the design.
A local influencer recommended a café where you can meet friends.
Tip: when that/which is the object, you can drop it: the desserts (that) I wanted to try. You cannot drop it when it is the subject: a video that showed the menu.
✔ The man who lives here
✘ The man which lives here
15Sentence building

Articles: a, an, the, or nothing

a / an = one, not special. the = we both know which one. No article = general plural or uncountable things.
UseExample
a / an: first mentionI saw a video about a small café.
the: second mention / specificThe café looked charming. The video persuaded me.
the: only onethe sun, the internet, the best part
no article: generalI enjoy music. Influencers help businesses.
no article: fixed phrasesgo to work, go shopping, go home, at night

a before a consonant sound (a café, a walk). an before a vowel sound (an hour, an easy programme).

✔ I went shopping and cooked dinner.
✘ I went the shopping and cooked the dinner.

В русском артиклей нет. Спросите себя: «Мы оба знаем, о каком именно предмете речь?» Да → the. Нет, первый раз → a.

ℹ️ How this test was designed (and its limits)

Established placement tests follow the same pattern, and this test copies it:

  • Cambridge English Placement Test: Reading, Use of English and Listening, reported as a CEFR level from Pre-A1 to C2 (Cambridge).
  • Oxford Placement Test: a Use of English section plus a Listening section, each with its own score and CEFR band (overview).
  • EF SET: reading and listening only, adaptive, with results aligned to the six CEFR levels (EF SET & CEFR).
  • Linguaskill: a fuller version that also adds writing and speaking, marked separately (Cambridge).
Limits: this is a quick, indicative check with 51 questions. It is not an official exam and not adaptive. Real tests use thousands of calibrated questions. It does not test speaking or writing. Use the result to guide teaching, and retest every few weeks.

How the level is calculated: each question has a hidden CEFR level (A2, B1, B2 or C1). A level counts as passed at 60% correct or more. Dinara’s level is the highest level she passes in a row. A “+” means she scored 35–59% on the next level up.

Listening: the three recordings are the British Council’s own LearnEnglish listening lessons (linked, not copied). The questions are written for this site and are not the British Council’s exercises.
